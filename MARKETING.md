# Marketing plan: your first restaurant at $1,000 a month

The product is the easy part. This is how you get the first "yes".

## 1. Who to sell to

Not every restaurant. You want places that are **packed on weekends but quiet on weekdays**. They already have the
customers; they just don't bring them back on slow nights.

How to find them in 20 minutes:

1. Open Google Maps and search `restaurants near me` or `burger restaurant <your city>`.
2. Click a place and scroll to **Popular times**. Look at Friday and Saturday, then Monday and Tuesday.
3. Keep the ones with: tall weekend bars and short weekday bars, **4.2+ stars**, and **300+ reviews** (proof they get
   traffic).
4. Skip big chains (they have head-office marketing) and places that already run a text club.

Paste the list into Claude:

> Here are restaurants I copied from Google Maps. Make a table: name, phone, rating, number of reviews, and which
> weekday looks quietest. Sort by number of reviews, highest first.

Aim for a list of 30.

## 2. The pitch (say it like a story)

Walk in on a quiet afternoon (2–4 pm, never during a rush) and ask for the owner or manager. Keep it under a minute:

> "Quick question: what does Monday night look like here?
>
> Here's what I do. I put a little card on every table: free appetizer, just scan this. Guests leave their number to
> get it, so you build your own list of customers. Nobody else owns it, not DoorDash, not anyone.
>
> Then on a slow night, I send one text to everyone: 2-for-1 burgers tonight. If ten tables of three come in, that's
> about twelve hundred dollars on a night that was empty.
>
> It's $1,000 a month. Can I show you what it looks like on your phone?"

Then show them the demo on your phone: the sign-up page with *their* restaurant name (set `RESTAURANT_NAME` before
you go), and the owner page writing a text.

## 3. Pricing

- **$1,000 a month**, month to month. Don't sell yearly; monthly is an easier yes.
- **Why they'll pay:** it can bring them about 10× that in extra sales. Two slow nights a week × ~$1,200 ≈ $10,000 a
  month. Say "about" and "can"; it's an example, not a promise.
- **If they hesitate:** offer the first month at $500 while the list grows, or "if the first two texts don't bring a
  single table, you don't pay the second month." Only offer what you're happy to honor.
- **Costs to you:** hosting ($0–7 a month), Twilio texts (about 1 cent each, so 2 texts a week to 500 people ≈ $40 a
  month) and Claude (cents). Include them in the $1,000, or bill them on top.

## 4. Your first 30 days

| Week | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Build the list of 30. Set up the demo with a real restaurant's name. Visit 10. |
| 2 | Visit the other 20. Follow up with everyone who said "maybe" (a text, then one more visit). |
| 3 | First client: print the cards, put them on every table, check the sign-up works from a guest's phone. |
| 4 | First slow-night text. Count the tables that night with the owner. Take a photo, ask for a short quote. |

That photo and quote is your best sales tool for restaurant number two.

## 5. Keep them (and get more)

- Send **two texts a week, max**. More than that and people reply STOP.
- Mix it up: slow nights, birthdays, new menu items, holidays, events.
- Every month, send the owner a one-line report: list size, texts sent, tables that came in.
- Ask for an introduction: owners know other owners.

## 6. What to say when they say no

- **"I already post on Instagram."** Instagram shows your post to a few people. A text gets read by almost everyone
  on the list, and you choose the night.
- **"Texts are spammy."** Only people who asked for it get them, and they can stop anytime. Two a week, with a real
  offer.
- **"Too expensive."** One full slow night pays for the month. Want to try it for a month and count the tables?

## The rules

Consent first, never buy numbers, every text has "Reply STOP to opt out", and no texts late at night. The details are
in the [README](README.md#the-rules-please-dont-skip). Follow them; it protects you and the restaurant.
